Regentra supports two ways to add a second factor to sign-in: **passkeys** (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello, or a security key) and **TOTP authenticator apps** (Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator, Authy, 1Password, etc.). Either one satisfies your MFA requirement — you don't need both.

## Who needs MFA

MFA is **mandatory for Admin, Super Admin, and MSP Admin roles**, regardless of whether your organization has turned on the org-wide MFA toggle. Every other role follows your organization's MFA setting, which an admin controls from **Settings → Authentication & SSO**.

<Warning>
  If you hold an admin-tier role, you can't opt out of MFA even if your organization's MFA toggle is off. Set up a passkey or TOTP before you're prompted at login.
</Warning>

## Setting up MFA

<Steps>
  <Step title="Navigate to security settings">
    Go to **Settings → Authentication & SSO**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a method">
    Set up a passkey under the **Passkeys** card, or a TOTP app under the **Multi-Factor Authentication** card. You can set up both if you like.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the factor">
    A passkey confirms itself the moment your device creates it (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello, or your security key's own prompt). A TOTP app confirms by scanning a QR code and entering the 6-digit code it generates.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Passkeys

Passkeys use WebAuthn/FIDO2 — the same technology behind "Sign in with Face ID" on other sites. They're phishing-resistant: a passkey is bound to Regentra's actual domain, so it can't be tricked into authenticating on a lookalike site the way a typed code can.

Enroll one from **Settings → Authentication & SSO → Passkeys**. You can register more than one — for example, your laptop's Touch ID and a physical security key as a backup — and each shows up as its own entry you can name and revoke independently.

<Tip>
  A passkey satisfies your MFA requirement on its own, without an authenticator app — signing in with a passkey is already two factors (something you have, something you are or know) in one step.
</Tip>

## Trusted devices

When you complete an MFA challenge, you can check **Remember this device for 14 days** to skip the second-factor prompt on that device until it expires. Manage which devices are currently trusted — and revoke any you don't recognize — from **Settings → Authentication & SSO → Trusted Devices**.

## Enrolling or removing a factor

Both enrolling a new factor and removing an existing one require you to have verified a factor earlier in your **current session** — a fresh MFA check, not just being logged in. If you're prompted to re-verify while trying to change your MFA settings, that's this step-up check; complete it and you'll return to where you were.

<Note>
  This applies even to removing your last remaining factor — you can't disable MFA on your account without proving you still control a factor first.
</Note>

## Recovery

When you enroll a TOTP authenticator, Regentra generates one-time backup codes — store them somewhere safe (a password manager, not the same device as your authenticator app). Each code works once.

If you lose access to your authenticator device and have a passkey enrolled, sign in with the passkey instead — no recovery needed. Otherwise:

1. **Use a backup code** at the MFA prompt to sign in.
2. **Ask an admin to reset your MFA** from **Settings → Team Members**, if you're out of backup codes too. This clears your enrolled factors so you can set up a new one on your next login.

## Password requirements

Regentra requires a minimum password length of **12 characters** with no other composition rule — no forced mix of uppercase, numbers, or symbols. Every password is checked against a breach database at signup, reset, and change; a password that's appeared in a known breach is rejected even if it meets the length requirement.

<Info>
  If the breach-check service is temporarily unreachable, Regentra doesn't block you from setting your password — it lets the change through and records the fact that the check couldn't run, rather than locking you out over a third-party outage.
</Info>

## Account lockout

After **5 failed sign-in attempts**, an account is locked for **15 minutes** before another attempt is allowed. This applies per account, not per IP address, so a locked-out user can't be unlocked simply by trying from a different network.

## Azure AD users and MFA

If your organization uses [Entra ID SSO](/settings/sso) for authentication, Regentra checks whether the sign-in response from Azure AD actually proves MFA happened, rather than assuming it did just because SSO was used. If Azure AD's conditional access enforced MFA on that sign-in, Regentra accepts it as satisfying its own requirement and skips its passkey/TOTP prompt. If it can't confirm that — Azure AD didn't enforce MFA, or the proof is missing — and this user still requires MFA, Regentra falls back to its own passkey or TOTP challenge right after the SSO sign-in.

<Info>
  Regentra doesn't duplicate MFA unnecessarily, but it also never blanket-trusts SSO as a substitute for MFA. Admin-tier roles always require MFA regardless of SSO — signing in via SSO satisfies that requirement only when Azure AD's own proof is strong enough; otherwise Regentra still prompts them.
</Info>
