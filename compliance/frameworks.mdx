Frameworks are the foundation of your compliance program in Regentra. Each framework represents a compliance standard — HIPAA, NIST CSF 2.0, SOC 2, ISO 27001, CMMC Level 1 and Level 2, PCI DSS, GDPR, FTC Safeguards, SEC Regulation S-P, or FINRA — with its own set of controls, requirements, and structure. Regentra supports 11 frameworks in total.

## Adopting a Framework

<Steps>
  <Step title="Navigate to the Frameworks page">
    Open the **Compliance** module and click **Frameworks** in the sidebar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Browse available frameworks">
    Each framework card shows the standard name, a brief description, and the number of controls it contains.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Adopt">
    Click the **Adopt** button on the framework you want to activate. A confirmation dialog explains what will be created.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the seeded controls">
    After adoption, navigate to the Controls page to see the full control set that was created for your organization.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What Happens When You Adopt

When you adopt a framework, Regentra performs several actions automatically:

1. **Controls are seeded** — the complete control set for that framework is created in your organization, each mapped to the relevant framework requirements
2. **CCF mappings are applied** — controls that overlap with previously adopted frameworks are linked through the Common Control Framework
3. **Gap analysis activates** — your compliance dashboard populates with a gap analysis showing which controls are Not Started, In Progress, or Implemented
4. **Framework-specific features unlock** — depending on the framework, additional tools become available

<Info>
  Controls seeded by framework adoption belong to your organization. You can edit their descriptions, add implementation notes, and customize them to fit your environment.
</Info>

## Switching Between Frameworks

If you have adopted multiple frameworks, use the **framework dropdown** in the sidebar to switch your view. This filters the Controls page, dashboard metrics, and reports to show only the selected framework.

You can also select **All Frameworks** to see a unified view of every control across your compliance program.

## Framework-Specific Features

Some frameworks unlock additional capabilities beyond standard controls:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="HIPAA">
    Regentra's HIPAA framework slug is **`hipaa-2026`**. It builds you to the Security Rule as proposed in the January 2025 NPRM (RIN 0945-AA22, 90 FR 898) — the standard most organizations will be audited against once OCR finalizes the rule. The 2026 target exceeds the currently-binding 2013 Omnibus Rule on most provisions, so meeting Regentra's checklist also meets today's enforceable requirements.

    - **NPRM banner** — every HIPAA framework view shows a banner identifying the 2026 NPRM scope and highlighting the 9 new mandatory controls (asset inventory, MFA mandate, vulnerability scanning, network segmentation, etc.)
    - **PARTIAL-coverage callout** — when a framework requirement is satisfied only by PARTIAL-strength mappings (no FULL-strength control covers it), the gap-analysis view shows a yellow banner so auditors are alerted that supplemental evidence will be requested
    - **Annex filter** — HIPAA-specific policy templates (Notice of Privacy Practices, Patient Rights, Minimum Necessary, etc.) are tagged `annex:hipaa` and can be filtered separately from general controls
    - **BAA Tracking** — maintain a register of Business Associate Agreements with vendors and partners
    - **Privacy Rule controls** — dedicated controls for patient data handling, minimum necessary standard, and individual rights (covers 37+ §refs the HHS SRA Tool's Security-Rule scope doesn't address)
    - **Breach Notification Rule** — full coverage of §164.402–414 (Four-Factor Test, Individual / Media / HHS notification, BA reporting)
    - **Security Risk Assessment** — structured SRA workflow with native [HHS/ONC SRA Tool v3.6.1 alignment](/compliance/risk-assessment#alignment-with-the-hhsonc-sra-tool) (40-threat catalog, per-§ref question reference, calibrated 3×3 risk matrix)
    - **regulatoryStatus tag per requirement** — power users tracking what's strictly binding today vs. proposed under the NPRM can filter by `binding-with-proposed-changes` vs `nprm-proposed-new`
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="SOC 2">
    - **Trust Service Criteria** — controls organized by the five TSC categories: Security (mandatory), Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy (optional based on scope)
    - **61 AICPA criteria** — complete coverage of CC1-CC9, A1, PI1, C1, and P1-P8 per the 2017 Trust Services Criteria with 2022 revised points of focus
    - **Control activity mapping** — link controls to specific TSC points for auditor readability
    - **Audit periods** — define observation windows for Type II assessments and track control effectiveness over time
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="CMMC">
    - **Maturity levels** — controls tagged by CMMC level (1 through 3) so you can focus on your target certification
    - **Practice mapping** — each control maps to specific CMMC practices and domains
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="NIST CSF">
    - **Function-based organization** — controls grouped under Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover
    - **Subcategory mapping** — granular mapping to NIST CSF subcategories and informative references
    - **CSF Tiers self-assessment** — a separate, org-level self-rating of how your organization governs and manages cybersecurity risk, distinct from individual control implementation status. See [CSF Tiers self-assessment](#csf-tiers-self-assessment) below.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="SEC Reg S-P">
    - **Safeguards and incident-response program controls** — the written information security and incident-response program requirements under 17 CFR 248 Subpart A
    - **Customer notice** — controls for the 30-day individual notification requirement following unauthorized access to sensitive customer information
    - **Vendor/service-provider notice** — the 72-hour notice obligation for third-party service providers
    - **Reg S-AM** — affiliate marketing notice-and-opt-out controls, where applicable
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="FINRA">
    - **Supervision** — written supervisory procedures (WSPs) and supervisory-control controls
    - **Records retention** — including WORM/17a-4 electronic recordkeeping requirements
    - **Communications capture** — controls for retaining and supervising business communications
    - **Cybersecurity program** — controls aligned to FINRA's cybersecurity and Reg S-ID (identity theft prevention) expectations
    - **Business continuity and AML** — BCP and anti-money-laundering program controls
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Framework-first presentation

When you open a control detail page with a specific framework selected, the page leads with that framework's own requirement text and reference numbers — a "**{Framework} Requirements Satisfied**" card shows the official clause or section language for every requirement the control maps to, along with whether the mapping is a Full or Partial match. Regentra's own internal control code and domain are still shown, but as a secondary line labeled as Regentra's canonical control — never presented as if it were part of the framework's own structure.

This means a reviewer who knows a specific framework cold — an auditor, a compliance officer preparing for a SOC 2 Type II, a HIPAA privacy officer — sees the language they expect first, with Regentra's cross-framework mapping layer available underneath it rather than in front of it.

## CSF Tiers self-assessment

For organizations that have adopted NIST CSF, the framework page includes a **CSF Tiers** card — a self-assessment of how your organization governs and manages cybersecurity risk, separate from the implementation status of any individual control.

Tiers are scored across two dimensions:

- **Cybersecurity Risk Governance**
- **Cybersecurity Risk Management**

For each dimension you record a **current tier** (required) and, optionally, a **target tier**, plus a rationale explaining the rating. Tiers range from **Partial** (Tier 1) through **Risk Informed**, **Repeatable**, to **Adaptive** (Tier 4).

<Note>
  NIST is explicit that Tiers are not maturity levels. A lower tier is not automatically "worse" — it describes how formal, integrated, and risk-informed your cybersecurity practices are today. Progress to a higher tier only when your risk exposure or a mandate calls for it.
</Note>

Saving a tier rating requires an Approver-level role. Each save records who assessed it and when, so the rating carries the same accountability as an approved policy.

## Multi-Framework Compliance

Regentra is designed for organizations that need to satisfy more than one framework simultaneously. Here is how multi-framework compliance works in practice:

- **Adopt as many frameworks as you need** — there is no limit on the number of active frameworks per organization
- **The CCF consolidates overlapping controls** — when two frameworks require the same security measure, you manage it as one control with mappings to both
- **Status and evidence propagate** — marking a shared control as Implemented updates its status across every mapped framework
- **Reports can be generated per framework** — even though controls are shared internally, reports are scoped to a single framework for auditor consumption

<Warning>
  Unadopting a framework removes it from your active view and dashboard but does not delete the underlying controls or evidence. You can re-adopt at any time to restore the framework view.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  If your client is preparing for both HIPAA and SOC 2, adopt both frameworks early. The CCF will show you exactly how much overlap exists, and you can prioritize shared controls to maximize progress across both standards at once.
</Tip>
