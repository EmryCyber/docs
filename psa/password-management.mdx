Securely store and manage customer credentials. Passwords are encrypted at rest with per-tenant encryption keys. Regentra checks your stored passwords against known breach lists and alerts you when a match is found.

## What it does

- **Encrypted storage** — Passwords are AES-256 encrypted at rest with per-organization keys
- **Secure sharing** — Generate an expiring, limited-view share link, optionally emailed or PIN-protected, instead of pasting a password into chat or a ticket
- **Access control** — Only team members with vault permissions can view passwords
- **Breach alerts** — Regentra monitors stored passwords against known breach databases and alerts you on match
- **Audit trail** — Every access to a password is logged for compliance
- **Three vaults** — a per-client vault for each [Company](/psa/companies-contacts), an org-wide **MSP Shared Vault** for your own internal credentials, and a **Personal Vault** visible only to the technician who created it

## How to manage passwords for a customer

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Password Vault">
    In the PSA module sidebar, click **Passwords**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a company">
    Click a company card to view all stored passwords. Empty companies show an empty state.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a new password">
    Click **Add Password**. Enter a label (e.g. "Admin Account", "Mail Server"), username, password, and optional URL or notes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a share link">
    Click the password entry's **Share** action to open **Create Share Link**. Choose a max view count (1/3/5/10), an expiry (1 hour up to 30 days), optionally an email address to send the link to, and optionally a 4-8 digit PIN. Click **Create Link** to get a one-time URL — the decryption key lives only in the URL fragment and is never sent to or stored on the server.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Security expectations

This vault is designed for MSP operational use: storing admin credentials, API keys, and service-account passwords your team needs to perform support work. **It is not a password manager for end-users** — don't store employee or customer personal passwords here.

**Access control:** Shared-vault access depends on your PSA role, the organization's vault permissions and each entry's visibility. Administrators configure technician access separately for the MSP and client vaults in **Settings → Password Vault**: view/reveal, share, add, edit, delete and folder management. Other actions require View. Personal entries belong to their creator.

Without a custom technician policy, the supported shared-vault actions are allowed, including add/edit/delete. Administrators can restrict them separately for MSP and client vaults. The entry's visibility still limits which records a technician can open.

Choose **All techs**, **Specific people**, or **Only me** for a shared entry. Specific people must be eligible active teammates. Only the creator, or an authorized recovery administrator after the creator is deactivated, can change that visibility. Entry lists omit records you cannot open rather than disclosing their names.

**Encryption:** Passwords are encrypted using AES-256 with per-organization master keys. Decryption happens in-memory only when a user explicitly views the password. Encrypted payloads are never logged or exposed in APIs.

**Breach monitoring:** Regentra continuously checks your stored passwords against publicly known breach databases. If a match is detected, an alert is generated in your dashboard and sent to vault admins. Reset the compromised credential immediately.

## Notes, editing and sharing

Reveal an entry to inspect its current password and notes; use Edit to change the stored record. Shared-vault actions appear only when your permissions allow them. If a save fails, review the displayed error before assuming the change was stored.

Changing a password revokes its existing share links so old links cannot keep disclosing a copied credential. Creating the same password again does not count as a rotation. Deleting an entry also stops its share links. If emailing a new share link fails, the link can still exist; the page reports the email failure separately.

## Authenticator codes

Where vault TOTP is enabled, add an authenticator setup key or an `otpauth://totp/` URI to an entry. The setup key is write-only after saving; replace it to change the configuration or remove it when it is no longer needed.

**Show TOTP code** requires entry access and a recent passkey or authenticator verification. Codes show their remaining lifetime and are hidden when you leave the window or after 30 seconds. A copied code can remain in clipboard history even after the display is hidden.

Supported configurations are SHA1/SHA256/SHA512, 6 or 8 digits, and 30- or 60-second periods. HOTP and arbitrary QR-image uploads are not supported. This capability is controlled by platform rollout; an unavailable code-generation feature does not make the stored password unavailable.

## Delete and restore

Deleted entries move to **Recently deleted** for 30 days. People with the required delete/read permissions can restore them while the window remains. Restoring does not reactivate old share links; create new links if needed.

After the restore window, the daily purge permanently removes the entry and its associated share/access records. The Recently deleted view shows the time remaining. If a company is purged, its vault entries are moved to the MSP vault rather than silently deleting operational credentials; their existing deleted/restore state is retained.

## Linking credentials to networks

Entries in the vault can be linked to a subnet or VLAN in [Networks](/psa/networks) — for example, the admin credential for a firewall or wireless controller. Rather than storing the secret again on the network record, you link out to the existing vault entry, so there's one credential, one owner, and one audit trail no matter where it's referenced from. When someone types something that looks like a password directly into a network's notes, Regentra flags it and offers to move it into the vault instead.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I export passwords from the vault?">
    No. Passwords cannot be bulk-exported for security reasons. Copy them one at a time via the UI.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens if a password is found in a breach database?">
    An alert is generated and sent to vault admins. The password is flagged in the UI as **Breached**. Reset it immediately. The alert remains visible until you acknowledge it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I set an expiration date on a password?">
    Not yet. You can add a manual expiration note in the notes field. Automatic expiration alerts are on the roadmap.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is the vault end-to-end encrypted?">
    Passwords are encrypted at rest. The connection is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Once decrypted server-side and shown in your browser, security depends on your browser and device. Never copy passwords into insecure channels (email, Slack, etc.).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
