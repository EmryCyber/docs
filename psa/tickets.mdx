Tickets are the core unit of work in Regentra's PSA. Every client interaction — whether it starts as an email, portal submission, live chat, or SMS — becomes a ticket that flows through a defined lifecycle.

## Ticket lifecycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open">
    Ticket is created and awaiting triage or assignment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="In Progress">
    A technician is actively working the ticket. SLA response timer stops when the first reply is sent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Waiting on Customer">
    The technician has replied and is awaiting a customer response. SLA resolution timer pauses in this state.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Waiting Approval">
    The ticket is on hold pending an [approval decision](#ticket-approvals) from a customer contact or another approver. SLA resolution timer pauses here too. This status can only be entered through the approval flow — it isn't a selectable choice in the manual status dropdown — though a technician can still manually move the ticket to a different status from the dropdown while an approval is pending or has expired.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Escalated">
    Ticket has been escalated to a senior technician or team lead due to complexity or SLA risk.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resolved">
    The issue is fixed. The ticket auto-closes after 24 hours if the customer does not reopen it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Closed">
    Terminal state — work is complete and SLA timers stop accruing. A technician can manually reopen a closed ticket from the status dropdown if the customer comes back. An inbound email reply always reopens the same closed ticket; an inbound SMS reply reopens it if it arrives within 30 minutes of closing, otherwise a new ticket is created; a portal reply on a closed ticket is added without reopening it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Resolved and Closed tickets display with muted status badges to visually distinguish them from active work.
</Note>

## Creating tickets

Tickets can be created through five channels:

| Channel | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| **Manual** | Technician creates a ticket from the PSA dashboard |
| **Email inbound** | Emails sent to your support address automatically create tickets |
| **Portal** | Customers submit tickets through the branded support portal |
| **Live chat** | Starting a chat in the customer portal creates a ticket immediately, with the opening message as its first reply |
| **SMS** | Inbound SMS messages create tickets (requires phone number configuration) |

When a technician creates a ticket manually, a [company](/psa/companies-contacts) is required — pick an existing one or create a new one on the spot (creating a new company counts against the Free-Tier client limit, same as adding one from the Companies page). The only exception is a ticket created from a compliance control's remediation link, which legitimately has no customer company. Typing a contact name and email that doesn't match an existing contact creates a real, findable [portal contact](/psa/companies-contacts) record under the resolved company — it's never a free-text stub on the ticket alone.

## Ticket detail page

Each ticket includes:

- **Replies** — Threaded conversation between technician and customer
- **Internal notes** — Private notes visible only to your team
- **Time entries** — Logged time with billable/non-billable designation
- **SLA badge** — Real-time SLA status (On Track, At Risk, Breached, Met)
- **Linked Asset** — One [asset](/psa/assets#linking-assets-to-tickets) associated with the ticket, scoped to the ticket's company
- **AI draft** — AI-generated reply suggestion based on ticket context and knowledge base articles

<Tip>
  The AI draft pulls from your knowledge base using RAG. The more KB articles you maintain, the better the draft quality.
</Tip>

## Priority is customer-controlled

The priority chosen at ticket creation is the source of truth across the system. The AI auto-analyzer reads priority for context and may suggest a different value (visible on the ticket sidebar), but it **cannot change `ticket.priority` on its own** — not up, not down. Customer-set CRITICAL stays CRITICAL.

Priority changes after creation happen only through:

- **Operator edit** on the ticket detail page
- **Operator-authored automation rules** (Settings → Automations) using the Change Priority action
- **Policy escalations** — VIP override, ticket-spike pattern, and AI security/frustration escalation can each bump LOW/MEDIUM → HIGH, never downgrade

See [Automations & AI](/psa/automations-and-ai#ai-and-ticket-priority) for the full matrix.

## Board view vs list view

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Board view">
    Kanban-style columns organized by ticket status. Drag tickets between columns to update status. Best for visual triage and daily standups.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="List view">
    A row-per-ticket layout, newest first, showing ticket number, priority, status, title, contact, and assignee. Best for scanning and filtered searches; combine with the filters below to narrow the list.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Filtering and search

Filter tickets by status, priority, assignee, client, SLA status, or date range. Combine filters to narrow results. Search (3+ characters) matches the ticket title, description, and the requester's name or email; a ticket number matches at any length.

The **All Tickets** stat card at the top of the list always shows your organization's total ticket count, regardless of any filters you have active. Clicking it clears every filter and the search box, taking you back to the fully unfiltered list. When filters are applied, a small "N matching" line under the card shows how many tickets match your current view.

## Auto-close behavior

Tickets in **Resolved** status automatically transition to **Closed** after 24 hours. If a customer replies to a resolved ticket within that window, the ticket reopens to **Open**.

## Master and child tickets

When the same incident affects several customers — a vendor outage, a regional ISP problem, a phishing wave — promote one ticket to a **master** and link the rest as children. The master's status, assignee, and resolution cascade to its children on demand: when you flip the master to RESOLVED or CLOSED, you're prompted to apply the same status to every linked child (resolved/closed children are left alone so a previously-fixed customer isn't yanked back into a queue).

Click **Promote to Master** in the ticket detail page's header toolbar to make a ticket a master, or **Link to Master** to attach it as a child of an existing master; a child can be unlinked from the same toolbar control. A master ticket's linked children are listed in the **Linked Children** panel on its detail page. Master and child tickets are first-class — they appear in board view, list view, reports, and SLA tracking like any other ticket.

Use cases:

- **Vendor outage**: open one master ticket against the vendor, link every customer ticket reporting the outage. One reply on the master can be cascaded to all children.
- **Phishing wave**: one master incident ticket; each reported phishing email becomes a child, so the response team works one timeline instead of dozens.
- **Site-wide maintenance**: master for the maintenance window, children for any customer who logs an issue during it — closes the loop when the window ends.

## Ticket approvals

Some work needs a customer's sign-off before you proceed — a parts purchase, a change window, anything above a client's comfort threshold. **Request approval** on the ticket detail page opens a short form:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose an approver">
    Pick from a list of the ticket's company's contacts, or enter a custom name and email. The contact matching the ticket's requester is preselected when there is one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe what needs approval">
    A short message — e.g. "Replacement webcam for Joe — $40" — gives the approver enough context to decide without seeing the rest of the ticket.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the request">
    The ticket status flips to **Waiting Approval** and the approver receives an email with **Approve** and **Deny** buttons — no login required.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The approver's decision page shows only the ticket title, number, the customer contact's name, the technician who requested approval, your organization name, and the message you wrote — never the rest of the ticket's contents. Once they decide, the ticket automatically returns to **Open** and the outcome is recorded on the ticket.

A few things worth knowing:

- Only one approval request can be pending on a ticket at a time.
- **Waiting Approval** pauses the SLA resolution clock, the same way Waiting on Customer does.
- The status can only be entered through the approval flow — it doesn't appear as a selectable choice in the manual status dropdown, and it can't be set by an automation rule. A technician can still manually move the ticket to a different status while an approval is pending.
- The approval link expires after 7 days. An expired, undecided request doesn't automatically change the ticket's status — a technician needs to change it manually, or send a new approval request (allowed again once the old one has expired).
- The whole flow is **requester-blind**: the customer who opened the ticket sees it as ordinary "In Progress" the entire time and receives no notification when the approval starts or ends. Only the designated approver is contacted.

## Ticket numbering

By default, tickets are numbered with a three-letter prefix derived from the customer's company name (e.g. `ACM-0001` for Acme Corp) so numbers are easy to recognize at a glance. You can change this under **Settings → Tickets → Ticket Numbering**:

- **Customer-derived** — the default described above.
- **Fixed prefix** — the same 1-8 character prefix on every ticket, regardless of company (e.g. `LIT-0001`).
- **No prefix** — just the number, with no letters.

You can also choose the number of digits the counter pads to (3-6 digits), and preview exactly how the next ticket number will look before saving.

If you're migrating from another PSA and want your numbering to continue where the old system left off, **Set Next Ticket Number** lets you jump the counter forward. This can only move the counter up, never back down, so you can't accidentally create duplicate or out-of-order ticket numbers.
